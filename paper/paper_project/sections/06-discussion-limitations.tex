\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}

The present scope is low-dimensional KRR with stationary kernels. The Fourier
grid grows exponentially with dimension, and a nonstationary kernel does not
give the same Toeplitz structure. A centered box is also best aligned with an
isotropic spectrum; anisotropic or irregular spectra may require a different
active set.

Active-box effectiveness depends on spectral alignment and coupling. The
score \(\rho_j\) uses the Fourier weights, \(N\), and \(\lambda\), but it does
not see the sample-dependent off-diagonal entries of \(G\). A useful box must
therefore contain the difficult directions while leaving a complement with
small diagonal error and weak coupling. The terms \(\delta_T\) and
\(\gamma_{BT}\) in \cref{prop:active-block-precond} retain these effects.

The end-to-end evidence is strongest relative to EFGP-CG, the model line that
the proposed pipeline directly accelerates.  Across
\(10^7\)--\(3\times10^8\) samples, Synthetic favors the inverse family
(\(5.36\)--\(8.52\times\) speedup), while Winnebago favors the active-box
EigenPro family (\(5.42\)--\(9.65\times\)).  Reporting those branches
separately avoids interpreting a data-dependent route switch as a sudden
scaling effect.  The secondary KRR comparison is deliberately more modest:
Nystr\"om exposes a visible speed--accuracy tradeoff at smaller scales, and
RPCholesky reaches its declared memory limit above \(10^7\) samples.  These
outcomes are reported rather than removed by a fixed relative-accuracy gate.

Preconditioning cannot remove the \(O(N)\) Fourier setup cost. It reduces the
number of products after the system is built, but the type-1 NUFFTs for the
Toeplitz generator and right-hand side remain. Once setup dominates, further
reductions in the iteration count have limited effect on total time.

The two empirical levels should nevertheless not be merged.  End-to-end KRR
timing includes each method's own algorithmic setup and solve, whereas the
controlled comparison gives all solvers one identical Fourier system.  In the
latter experiment the frozen default is \(4.42\times\) faster than CG, so the
gain is not solely a setup effect.  The full-grid spectral member reaches a
\(5.13\times\) speedup and is the fastest family member on that frozen system.
This is the high-budget extreme of the proposed construction rather than an
external algorithm defeating it.  Localization should therefore be viewed as
a data-dependent construction/application tradeoff: the default limits setup
and storage, while the full-grid member can be stronger when the difficult
subspace is global.  The active inverse is slower in total despite its short
solve because factor construction dominates.

The reported end-to-end total is method-owned algorithmic training time, not
process wall clock.  Common data I/O, backend creation, and host-to-device
staging are excluded, and prediction is separate.  The fixed-system total
similarly excludes the one \(0.606\)-second shared construction of
\(A\) and \(\bvec\).  These accounting choices expose the setup and solver
effects but should be preserved when reproducing or comparing the numbers.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

A fixed Fourier-discretized KRR system can be severely ill-conditioned even
when each matrix-vector product is fast.  The proposed active inverse and
leading-eigenpair corrections reduce the solve cost when difficult directions
are concentrated in a Fourier box and its coupling to the complement is
small.  Combined with the binned setup, the inverse and eigenpair branches
give up to \(8.52\times\) and \(9.65\times\) complete-pipeline speedup over
EFGP-CG, respectively, while their time--accuracy outcomes remain explicit.
The identical-\(A,\bvec\) experiment confirms a \(4.42\times\)
solver-total gain for the default and a \(5.13\times\) gain for the full-grid
family member.  Comparisons with Jacobi, Nystr\"om, RPCholesky, and the
different spectral-family budgets delimit the claim: the family substantially
accelerates the EFGP-CG line, while the preferred member depends on the cost of
localization, factorization, and global eigenspace construction.
